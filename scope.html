Research Website

Scope

The purpose of this study is to determine and compare the effects of moringa (M. oleifera), tamarind (T. indica), and jackfruit (A. heterophyllus) seed extracts on water turbidity (NTU), dissolved oxygen (mg/L), and pH when used in solar water disinfection of Poso water (manual water pump), with a treatment duration of 6 hours, while using positive control (distilled water) and negative control (untreated poso water) setups.

Limitations

Despite careful planning, the study has several that may affect the results and interpretation. First, the six-hour testing duration may not capture long-term changes or stability in water quality following treatment. Environmental factors, such as variations in sunlight intensity, temperature, and weather conditions during SODIS exposure, are beyond the control of the researchers and may influence the effectiveness of the seed extracts. Additionally, the study is constrained by the available laboratory equipment, materials, and resources provided by the school, which may limit the precision and scope of measurements capabilities and experience of the research proponents also contribute to potential variability in data collection and analysis. Finally, while the study evaluates water quality indicators associated with possible potable water, it does not include microbiological testing, which is essential for confirming actual potability according to health standards. These limitations may be lessened or narrowed by conducting repeated trials under similar environmental conditions, following standardized laboratory procedures, and carefully monitoring and controlling experimental variables to ensure consistency and reliability of results.

Significance of the Study

This research holds importance for several related reasons that address both environmental sustainability and public health challenges, especially in local communities within resource-limited areas. Access to clean and safe drinking water remains a global challenge, with millions of people, including households relying on manual pumps, depending on untreated or inadequately treated water sources. This study directly attempts to solve this problem by improving water quality through investigating naturally, locally available seeds as water treatment agents. By investigating how moringa (M. oleifera), tamarind (T. indica), and jackfruit (A. heterophyllus) seed extracts affect important water quality variables, such as turbidity, pH, and dissolved oxygen, the research provides environmental researchers with data on natural alternatives to chemical coagulants, which may reduce health risks from waterborne diseases and contamination and help provide safe drinking water for most communities. This research supports SDG 6 (Clean Water and Sanitation) by exploring affordable, locally-accessible methods to improve water quality and reduce waterborne diseases in local communities with limited access to clean and well-treated water. Additionally, it helps SDG 13 (Climate Action) by using solar power and planting seeds instead of chemicals, which reduces energy use and helps protect the environment.
